UX Case Study JLR · Range Rover & Land Rover
April 2026 – Present

Approved Used

JLR · UX/UI Designer · Accenture · April 2026 – Present

JLR had a growing backlog of approved used stock. With retailers unable to bring in new vehicles until existing inventory moved, shifting approved used became a commercial priority — not just a UX one. My role was to audit the current digital entry points across Range Rover and Land Rover, understand why users weren't engaging with approved used, and propose changes grounded in data.

UX Audit Analytics Competitor Analysis CX Strategy Heatmapping

The Brief

"Assess the current entry points and identify opportunities to boost visibility primarily across Range Rover and Land Rover DX sites."

The Problem

JLR's approved used backlog was blocking retailers from taking in new stock. To unlock the pipeline, they needed to sell more approved used vehicles — which meant making that option visible and compelling to users who were arriving on new car pages.

Key Findings

—
Significant crossover between new and approved used audiencesA meaningful proportion of users arriving on new car pages were already considering approved used — the opportunity wasn't being captured.
—
New car entry points drove more approved used sessions than dedicated DXUsers were arriving at approved used via new car pages far more than via dedicated approved used entry points.
—
Drop-off between PLP and PDP was a key point of failureUsers browsing approved used listings weren't converting through to product pages — pointing to a gap in perceived value at the listing level.

Recommendations

1
Build a considered journey into approved used BuiltRather than dropping CTAs into the new car flow, guide users through the value of approved used first — why it exists, what they get — then route them to the order site once they're informed and bought in.
2
Combine new & approved used on results pages BuiltReduce the cognitive switch required — surface both options together so users can make an active, informed choice rather than having to navigate away.
3
Surface vehicle value on PDP earlier ExperimentalLead with specs, features, and selling points higher in the page — give users a reason to commit before they reach the CTA.

Outcome

2 of 3 shipped

Recommendations 1 and 2 were adopted and built into the Range Rover and Land Rover sites — the considered journey (signpost, extended Discovery component, ignite-bar entry) and the combined results page, starting with the contained, measurable phase. Recommendation 3 was positioned deliberately as an experiment to widen the client’s thinking beyond entry points, and remains open.

What I took from it. Anchoring every change to a number the client already trusted made the recommendations easy to say yes to, and sequencing them from lowest risk upwards meant the structural idea never had to win on its own. Next time I’d pair the analytics with a small round of user interviews earlier — the data showed where users switched, not why.

UX · Full Site Build NHN Horizon
Commodity Trading · Live

NHN Horizon

Commodity Trading · UX, UI & Front-end Build

A full website design and build for a commodity trading company operating across two distinct markets — agriculture, sourcing from Northern Nigeria and East Africa for wholesale buyers in the Middle East, and oil & gas in the Nigerian market. Competitors in this space run static, linear sites with a formal, information-led tone, and the site needed to sit credibly in that landscape while giving each audience a tailored experience without fragmenting the brand. I took it from positioning and competitor research through to a Figma design system and a React build. NHN Horizon is a venture of my own, which meant the commercial trade-offs were mine to make as well as the design ones.

UX Strategy Brand Design Competitor Research Figma → React Dual Audience
View live site

The Problem

Wholesale buyers in commodity trading decide whether a supplier is credible within seconds of landing on their site — and they expect the conventions of the sector. NHN Horizon serves two very different audiences, agriculture and oil & gas, from one small company. The site had to establish trust with both without looking like two businesses, and it had to work as the front door for outbound outreach, where a prospect arrives from an email with no prior context.

The Challenge

—
Design language constrained by conventionCompetitors in commodity trading run static, linear sites with a formal, information-led tone. NHN Horizon needed to sit comfortably in that landscape — familiar enough to build trust, considered enough to stand out.
—
One brand, two audiencesAgriculture and oil & gas clients have different expectations, vocabularies, and trust signals. The site needed to feel native to both without splitting into two separate products.
—
Competitor research across both sectorsBenchmarked trading platforms in agriculture and energy to understand conventions, identify gaps, and inform the visual and structural decisions.
—
Designing for the outreach journey, not just the browseMost first visits come from outbound emails to prospective buyers, so each sector’s landing view had to answer “who are you, what do you trade, and why should I reply?” above the fold — before any navigation.

Outcome

Live & in use

Shipped end to end — designed in Figma, built in React, and live as the front door for the company’s outreach to wholesale buyers, with each sector given its own entry point and visual register within one brand.

What I took from it. Designing for two audiences forced a discipline I now apply everywhere: decide what is shared and what is sector-specific before drawing anything, or the two experiences drift apart. Building it myself also changed how I hand over to developers — I now know which design decisions are cheap and which quietly cost a week.

How I work

Reasons, not opinions.

Start from the number
Session data, heatmaps and click share before any layout. If I can’t point to what a change is meant to move, it isn’t ready to be proposed.
Sequence by risk
Ship the contained, measurable change first and let it earn the structural one. Clients say yes to a small step far more readily than to a big idea.
Design inside the brand
Strict guidelines are a constraint I like. The craft is making every asset feel native to the brand, with copywriters, social and brand teams in the room from the start.
Build it, or build for it
Having taken a site from Figma to React myself, I hand over in components and states rather than pictures, and I know which decisions are cheap and which cost a sprint.
