UX Case Study JLR · Range Rover & Land Rover
April 2026 – Present

Approved Used

JLR · UX/UI Designer · Accenture · April 2026 – Present

JLR had a growing backlog of approved used stock. With retailers unable to bring in new vehicles until existing inventory moved, shifting approved used became a commercial priority — not just a UX one. My role was to audit the current digital entry points across Range Rover and Land Rover, understand why users weren't engaging with approved used, and propose changes grounded in data.

UX Audit Analytics Competitor Analysis CX Strategy Heatmapping

The Brief

"Assess the current entry points and identify opportunities to boost visibility primarily across Range Rover and Land Rover DX sites."

The Problem

JLR's approved used backlog was blocking retailers from taking in new stock. To unlock the pipeline, they needed to sell more approved used vehicles — which meant making that option visible and compelling to users who were arriving on new car pages.

Key Findings

—
Significant crossover between new and approved used audiencesA meaningful proportion of users arriving on new car pages were already considering approved used — the opportunity wasn't being captured.
—
New car entry points drove more approved used sessions than dedicated DXUsers were arriving at approved used via new car pages far more than via dedicated approved used entry points.
—
Drop-off between PLP and PDP was a key point of failureUsers browsing approved used listings weren't converting through to product pages — pointing to a gap in perceived value at the listing level.

Recommendations

1
Build a considered journey into approved usedRather than dropping CTAs into the new car flow, guide users through the value of approved used first — why it exists, what they get — then route them to the order site once they're informed and bought in.
2
Combine new & approved used on results pagesReduce the cognitive switch required — surface both options together so users can make an active, informed choice rather than having to navigate away.
3
Surface vehicle value on PDP earlierLead with specs, features, and selling points higher in the page — give users a reason to commit before they reach the CTA.

Commodity Trading · UX Design

A full website design for a commodity trading company operating across two distinct markets — agriculture and oil & gas. The client had strict requirements for the design language to feel familiar to their industry: competitors in this space typically run static, linear sites with a formal, information-led tone. The core challenge was honouring that brief whilst delivering one unified platform that gave each audience a genuinely tailored experience without fragmenting the brand.

UX Strategy Web Design Competitor Research Dual Audience Brand Design
View live site

The Brief

"Build a single platform for a commodity trading company that provides two distinct experiences — one for agriculture, one for oil and gas — whilst feeling native to the industry."

The Challenge

—
Design language constrained by conventionCompetitors in commodity trading run static, linear sites with a formal, information-led tone. The client needed to sit comfortably in that landscape — familiar enough to build trust, considered enough to stand out.
—
One brand, two audiencesAgriculture and oil & gas clients have different expectations, vocabularies, and trust signals. The site needed to feel native to both without splitting into two separate products.
—
Competitor research across both sectorsBenchmarked trading platforms in agriculture and energy to understand conventions, identify gaps, and inform the visual and structural decisions.

Experience 01

Agriculture

Grounded in trust and provenance — an earthy, assured visual language that speaks the language of agricultural commodity traders. Warm tones, clear market information, and a tone of reliable expertise.
